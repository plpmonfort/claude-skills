# Sustainability Rundown

A briefing for someone who does this for a living. Assume deep familiarity with
building physics, energy modelling, and services design. No primers, no
explaining what embodied carbon is.

## How this fits with my other skills

- Inherit every accuracy rule from general-research: search first, prefer primary
  sources, never guess, flag uncertainty, date anything that changes.
- Override its output format only. Use the structure below, links inline.
- A specific question about one standard or metric is general-research.
  AI news is ai-rundown. A broad catch-up on this field is this skill.

## Gathering

- Never answer from memory. Regulation and rating systems change constantly and
  stale guidance here is worse than useless — it's professionally risky.
- Search each area separately rather than one broad query.
- Default window is the last 90 days. This field moves in quarters, not weeks.
- Go to primary sources: USGBC and GBCI, IWBI, BREEAM, UKGBC, LETI, CIBSE,
  RIBA, government consultation pages, IEA, IPCC, UNEP, and tool developers'
  own release notes and changelogs.
- Distinguish clearly between: consultation open, consultation closed,
  legislation passed, and in force. These are routinely conflated in reporting
  and the difference is what determines whether I need to act.

## What counts as relevant

Rank by how much it changes what I do or specify:

1. Regulation and compliance affecting UK and EU practice — Part L and Part O,
   Future Homes Standard, EPBD, whole life carbon policy, GLA and local
   authority requirements, CSRD and disclosure rules.
2. Rating systems — LEED and its credit revisions via GBCI, WELL via IWBI,
   BREEAM, NABERS UK, plus scheme version releases and transition deadlines.
3. Tools and methods — Ladybug Tools, ClimateStudio, IES VE, EnergyPlus,
   Radiance, OpenStudio: releases, deprecations, validation studies, meaningful
   methodology changes.
4. Standards and benchmarks — CIBSE TM and AM guidance, LETI targets,
   RIBA 2030 Challenge, ASHRAE, weather file and climate projection updates.
5. Wider context — climate science, energy transition, materials and resource
   constraints, biodiversity policy — but only where it plausibly reaches
   project work within a year or two.

Leave out: corporate net zero pledges, awards, conference announcements,
vendor marketing, generic ESG commentary, and anything that doesn't change a
decision I'd make on a project.

## Output format

```
## Sustainability rundown — <today's date>

### Regulation and compliance
- Headline. What it changes, and when it bites. [link]

### Rating systems
- Headline. What it changes. [link]

### Tools and methods
- Headline. What it changes. [link]

### Wider picture
- Headline. Why it reaches practice. [link]

### Worth acting on
- One line, or omit this section entirely.
```

## Rules

- Twelve bullets maximum across the whole thing. Cut the weakest rather than
  exceeding it.
- Omit any section with nothing real in it. Do not pad.
- One to two lines per bullet. Every bullet carries a link.
- Always give effective dates and transition deadlines where they exist. A
  change with no date attached is not useful to me.
- Where something is proposed rather than confirmed, say so explicitly.
- If a quarter was quiet, say so. Never manufacture significance.
- No opening or closing summary.
- Never use bold for emphasis inside bullet points.
